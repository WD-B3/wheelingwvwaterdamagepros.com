Preventing Water Damage When You Leave Home in Wheeling, WV

By the Wheeling WV Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

A house left alone for a week has no one to catch a small leak. These steps cut the odds.

☎ (740) 231-9132.

Why leaving the house is a water risk

Most large water losses are made worse by time. A supply line that fails on a Tuesday morning when someone is home is a nuisance. The same failure on a Saturday when the family has left for a week can soak several floors. The goal of travel prep is to remove the water source or put someone in a position to notice a problem.

The steps below are cheap and quick. Not all apply to every house.

Before you go: a checklist

  1. Know where the main water valve is and that it turns fully. A stuck valve is a project to fix before you leave..
  2. For trips longer than a few days, consider shutting off the main valve and opening a faucet to drain the lines..
  3. Check washing machine hoses for bulges, cracks or rust, and shut their valves..
  4. Look under sinks and behind the toilet for damp spots..
  5. Empty and clear the refrigerator ice maker line if you will be gone a long time..
  6. Clean gutters and make sure downspouts carry water away from the foundation..
  7. Check the sump pump by pouring water into the pit, and test the battery backup if you have one..
  8. Ask a neighbor or friend to check the house and give them the number of your plumber..

Winter travel adds a freeze risk

If you leave in cold weather, the heat setting matters. Do not turn the heat completely off. Many plumbers suggest a minimum temperature well above freezing, and your own policy may require heat to be maintained. Open cabinet doors on exterior walls, insulate exposed lines and drain outdoor spigots. For more detail, see the frozen pipe page, which covers the thaw and the cleanup.

Power outages can shut heat off while you are away. A smart thermostat or a leak sensor that sends an alert to your phone can warn you in time.

Sensors are cheap compared with a flooded floor, but they do need batteries and a working internet connection to be useful.

Who should have a key

Choose one person who can enter the house, knows where the main valve is and has your contact and plumber numbers. Ask them to check for sounds, smells, damp spots and ceiling stains. Give them permission to shut the main valve. If they find water, they should call you, then the dispatch line at (740) 231-9132 for water removal.

A short checklist before you leave

  1. Shut off the main water valve if you will be gone more than a few days..
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the lines if the house will be cold..
  3. Set the thermostat to a safe minimum, not off, in winter..
  4. Check the sump pump and make sure the discharge line is clear..
  5. Turn off the water supply to the washing machine and ice maker..
  6. Ask a neighbor or relative to look in on the house during the trip..

The list takes about fifteen minutes and covers the most common causes of a loss while no one is home. A burst supply line can run for days unnoticed, and the damage grows with every hour.

Smart sensors and simple monitoring

Low cost water sensors can sit on the floor near a water heater, a washer or a sump pit and send an alert to your phone if they get wet. Some systems also close the main valve when a leak is detected. These tools do not replace good maintenance, but they shorten the time a leak runs before someone notices.

If you cannot arrange sensors, a trusted neighbor with a key is still one of the best protections. Leave your phone number and ask them to call you if they see anything unusual.

Winter travel in the Ohio Valley

Cold snaps in the upper Ohio Valley can drop temperatures quickly, and a house left cold is a house where pipes in outside walls can freeze. Before a winter trip, open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so warm air can reach the pipes. Keep the thermostat at a safe minimum and ask someone to check the house if a hard freeze is forecast.

The frozen pipe page explains what to do if a line has already frozen, including why you should shut off the main before you try to thaw anything.

Local note for Wheeling, WV

For freeze and rainfall numbers in the Wheeling area, rely on NOAA and the National Weather Service. The City of Wheeling Water Department notes that emergencies should go to 911, and it offers a leak and break reporting page for the public side of the system. If you are away and a neighbor sees water coming from the street or the meter, they can report it to the department.

Travelers from Triadelphia and Wellsburg often have a longer drive back after a cold snap, so a neighbor with a key is more valuable there.

Common questions

Should I shut off the water when I go away?

For longer trips it is a common precaution. For short trips, it depends on your comfort level and your plumbing. Ask a plumber if you are unsure.

What temperature should I leave the heat at in winter?

Many plumbers suggest well above freezing. Check your policy for requirements.

Do leak sensors work?

They can, if they have working batteries and an alert path. Test them from time to time.

Is there a Wheeling-specific number to report a street leak?

The City of Wheeling Water Department has a page for reporting a water leak or break. Emergencies go to 911.
