Permits for Water Damage Repair in Wheeling, WV: What to Know

By the Wheeling WV Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

Drying a wall usually does not need a permit. Rebuilding one with new wiring or plumbing often does. Here is what the city says.

☎ (740) 231-9132.

What the City of Wheeling says

According to the City of Wheeling Building Code Services page, a permit is required for any structural, electrical, plumbing or building alteration in the city. Applicants submit an application, a drawing, a materials list and a scope of work, along with the fee, and the project is reviewed for building code compliance. The permit must be on display at the work location until the project is complete.

The page also says that if you hire a contractor, the contractor is usually responsible for submitting the application materials, and it advises involving the Inspection Division early in a project to potentially reduce time, cost and delays.

What that means for water damage work

The rule is written broadly. It does not list every water damage task, and a complete list is on the city's own site. In general terms, the repair steps below are the ones to ask about before they start.

Do not assume. If your repair goes beyond drying and cosmetic finishes, call the office and ask.

Who files the permit

The city says the contractor is usually responsible for submitting the permit application materials. Ask the contractor who will file, what documents are needed and how long the review takes. If you are doing the work yourself, you will need to submit the materials.

Keep the permit posted at the work site, as the city requires. Inspections may be needed before walls are closed up, so ask when inspectors need to see the work.

Floodplain questions

The city lists a Floodplain Manager and a Flood Plain Map in the Building Code Services office. If your property is in a mapped flood area, repairs after flood damage may have extra requirements, and rules can be tied to how much damage the building suffered. Ask the floodplain manager before major rebuild work.

The flood damage page explains the cleanup side. The permit side is the city's.

Emergency work

Stopping water, removing standing water and starting drying are emergency steps and are typically done right away. Paperwork for later repairs can follow. Document what you do and keep receipts. If you are unsure, tell the Building Code Services office what you are doing and ask whether anything needs to be filed. The water extraction page covers the first steps.

Outside the city limits

The Wheeling rule applies inside the City of Wheeling. Neighboring towns and the county may have different rules, or none. Ask the town or city office where you live, or the county if you are in an unincorporated area.

Questions to ask the Building Code Services office

  • Is a permit needed for the specific repairs I plan?.
  • What documents do you need, and in what format?.
  • What is the fee and how long does review take?.
  • When will an inspector need to see the work?.
  • Does my property fall in a mapped flood area, and does that change the rules?.

Write down the answers, the name of the person you spoke with and the date. If a contractor is handling the permit, ask for a copy of the application and the approved permit.

Why this matters for insurance

Insurers often ask for proof that repairs were done properly. A permit and a passed inspection are good evidence. Skipping a required permit can complicate a claim, and it can cause problems when you sell the home later. If you are unsure whether a repair needs a permit, asking before you start is far cheaper than fixing it afterward.

A realistic timeline

Permit review takes time, and water damage does not wait. Plan the work in two tracks. Track one is mitigation: stopping the water, removing it and drying the structure. Track two is repair: the rebuild, which may need a permit. Run track one right away and use the drying days to gather the drawing, materials list and scope that the city asks for.

Ask the Building Code Services office how long review usually takes and whether a simple repair can be handled faster than a large rebuild. Then give your contractor the dates, so the crew does not close up walls before an inspection that may be required.

If an inspector needs to see framing, wiring or plumbing, leave the wall open until the inspection is done. Closing it early can mean cutting it open again, which costs time and money.

Local note for Wheeling, WV

Our research found the City of Wheeling's own pages for permit rules, but we did not find equivalent pages with the same detail for each nearby town. For Benwood, Moundsville and Wellsburg, call your city office and ask what is required for plumbing, electrical and structural repairs.

Ask them in the same way. What kind of work needs a permit, who files it and how long does review take.

Common questions

Do I need a permit to dry out my basement?

Drying is usually not an alteration, but confirm with Building Code Services if you plan to remove structure or change plumbing or wiring.

Who pulls the permit if I hire a contractor?

The city says the contractor is usually responsible for submitting the application materials.

What documents does the city ask for?

An application, a drawing, a materials list and a scope of work, along with the fee.

Does a permit rule in Moundsville match Wheeling's?

Not necessarily. Each city or town sets its own rules, so ask the Moundsville city office.
