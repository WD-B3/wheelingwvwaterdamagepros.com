Elm Grove and the Forks of Wheeling

Elm Grove is a neighborhood in the City of Wheeling, about 3.6 miles east of the Ohio River according to the city's 2021 historic survey. Big Wheeling Creek flows in from the south, and Little Wheeling Creek joins it at the historic Forks of Wheeling. The National Road, now US 40, runs through the community and turns sharply where the two creeks meet.

That setting shapes the water problems residents describe. Creek water can enter a building from the outside, while a split supply line or a failed appliance hose does the same damage from the inside. Water damage restoration in Elm Grove covers both, and the first questions are the same. Where is the water coming from, how deep is it, and is it safe to be near.

Housing in the neighborhood

Elm Grove has a long record as a community. It was incorporated as a town in 1890 and annexed to Wheeling in 1920. The city survey also documents mid century growth. Elm Terrace, a National Homes development built with prefabricated components, dates mainly to the 1950s and 1960s. Mil Acres, built on a former farm, has most of its houses from the same decades. The survey recommended both as potential National Register historic districts.

Different building periods dry differently, and a crew will want to know which one you live in. Ask whoever visits to note the flooring, the wall finish and what sits behind it before anything is removed, so the record matches what was actually in the home.

Creek flooding: what the record shows

  • A July 1888 storm dropped roughly eight inches of rain in a few hours. The city survey says creeks overflowed, railroad bridges washed away and several frame homes and businesses were destroyed..
  • On June 14, 2025, flash flooding hit the Wheeling area. The Herald-Star reported Wheeling Creek flowing over the Shilling Bridge in Elm Grove, and FOX Weather reported many Elm Grove businesses submerged..
  • The National Weather Service said one gauge near Valley Grove recorded 3 inches of rain in 62 minutes, and Ohio County totals may have approached 4 inches..

These are documented events in and around Elm Grove. They do not mean every street floods, and they say nothing about a particular address. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your own property.

After creek water: safety before cleanup

Floodwater is not clean water. It can carry sewage, fuel and sharp debris, and it may hide electrical hazards. Stay out of standing water that could touch outlets, cords or appliances. Keep children and pets away. Do not run household fans or an ordinary vacuum on a flooded floor, and do not try to dry out contaminated materials with household equipment.

  1. Leave the building if the water is rising or you smell gas, and call 911 for an immediate danger..
  2. Shut off the water supply only if it is safe and reachable..
  3. Do not touch the electrical panel while standing in water. Call a licensed electrician or the utility..
  4. Photograph visible damage from a safe spot before anything moves..
  5. Call for help before you begin removing wet materials..

Because floodwater is treated as contaminated under the ANSI/IICRC S500 water categories, cleanup uses protective gear and removal of porous materials such as carpet and pad. The flood damage page explains the process.

Leaks that have nothing to do with the creek

Not every Elm Grove call involves flooding. A washer hose can fail, a water heater can leak and a pipe can freeze and split in an unheated space. These losses usually involve cleaner water and a smaller area, so water extraction and structural drying are the main steps. Dry-looking walls can still hold moisture, which is why readings matter more than touch.

Insurance paperwork

Coverage depends on the cause of the damage and on your policy. Many homeowners policies treat flood from rising water differently from a sudden pipe break, and flood coverage is often a separate policy. Ask your insurer which applies. Photos, a dated list of damaged items and the moisture readings from a restoration crew are useful records. We do not decide coverage and we do not promise what a claim will pay.

Local note for Elm Grove

Most of the neighborhood facts on this page come from the City of Wheeling's 2021 Elm Grove survey, the National Weather Service event summary for June 14 and 15, 2025, and local news reporting on that flood. Countywide figures from that event, such as the nine deaths and roughly 350 homes with major damage reported by Ohio County emergency management, describe the whole county and not Elm Grove alone.

Questions from Elm Grove residents

Is Elm Grove part of the City of Wheeling?

Yes. The city's historic survey says Elm Grove was annexed to Wheeling in 1920, and the city lists it in its 6th Ward.

Does creek water count as clean water?

No. Floodwater is treated as contaminated, so cleanup uses protective methods and removes porous materials.

Can I dry out a flooded basement with fans?

Not safely when the water may be contaminated. Call for help and keep out of standing water near electrical equipment.

Is my Elm Grove address in a FEMA flood zone?

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and enter your address.

Does a standard homeowners policy cover flood?

Many do not. Flood is often a separate policy, so read yours and ask your insurer.
