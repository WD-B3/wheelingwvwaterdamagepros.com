How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your Wheeling, WV House

By the Wheeling WV Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

Most hidden leaks give small signs for weeks. Here is how to catch them with a meter, a flashlight and your nose.

☎ (740) 231-9132.

Start with the water meter

The easiest test for a hidden leak is the water meter. Turn off every faucet, appliance and fixture in the house. Then find the meter, which in many homes is in the basement or in a box near the street, and look at the dial or the small leak indicator. If it moves with everything off, water is going somewhere.

For a more careful test, note the reading, wait an hour without using water, then read it again. A change means a leak. If the meter is still moving after you shut the main valve at the house, the leak may be on the supply line between the meter and the house.

Small checks done twice a year catch a lot. Make it a habit when you change the clocks.

Signs inside walls and floors

Many leaks are hidden in walls. Look for paint that bubbles or peels, wallpaper that lifts, drywall that feels soft, baseboards that darken at the bottom or a smell like wet cardboard. Warm spots on a floor can point to a hot water line leak under a slab.

Sound is useful too. Quiet the house and listen for hissing or running water near walls. If you hear it when everything is off, a pipe is probably leaking.

Tools that help

  • A flashlight and a mirror for looking behind and under things..
  • A moisture meter, which can be bought for a modest price at hardware stores..
  • A thermal camera, which shows temperature differences from wet areas..
  • Food coloring for toilet tank tests..
  • A notebook to record meter readings and dates..

Moisture readings are not a number that means the same thing in every material. The same reading in drywall and in wood mean different things, so compare it to a dry area of the same material.

What to do once you find it

Shut off the water to the leaking line if you can, take photos and call a plumber. If materials are wet, dry them right away. Even a small leak can leave moisture in a wall or floor, and drying matters as much as repairing the pipe. The wall and drywall page explains how wet drywall is evaluated.

If you suspect a leak below a concrete floor, see the slab leak page for the signs.

Signs that point to a leak behind a wall or under a floor

Hidden leaks leave clues long before water shows. Paint that bubbles, wallpaper that lifts, a floor that feels soft or a baseboard that gaps from the wall can all point to moisture. A musty smell in a closet or cabinet is another warning, especially when the room has not been wet in an obvious way.

Sound helps too. With everything off, stand near a wall or a floor and listen for a faint hiss or drip. Check the cabinet under each sink, the area behind the toilet and the floor around the washer. Small drips there are the most common hidden source in older homes.

  • Warm spots on a floor can point to a leak in a hot water line..
  • A damp smell in the basement after dry weather can point to a line, not rain..
  • Ceiling stains that grow after someone uses a bath above often trace to a drain or supply fitting..

When to bring in a professional

A moisture meter and a thermal camera can show how far water has traveled without cutting into a wall. If you find damp materials, a plumber can find and fix the leak, and a restoration crew can dry the structure. Both steps are needed. Drying without fixing the source only delays the next problem.

The slab leak page explains the signs of a leak under a concrete floor, which is hard to see without special tools.

Local note for Wheeling, WV

The City of Wheeling Water Department is part of Public Works and manages a system with over 189 miles of transmission and distribution pipelines, according to the city. The department has a page for reporting a water leak or break, and emergencies are directed to 911. If you suspect the leak is on the street side, in the main or at the meter, report it to the department. Where its responsibility ends and yours begins is a good question to ask when you call.

Homeowners in Glen Dale and Benwood have their own city water departments, so call the one that serves your address.

Common questions

How do I read a water meter?

Look for the small dial or triangle that spins when water moves. With everything off, it should be still. Note the reading, wait, and read again.

Can a leak go undetected for months?

Yes. Slow leaks inside walls, floors and ceilings can run for a long time before stains appear.

Who do I call, a plumber or a restoration company?

Both, if materials are wet. A plumber fixes the pipe, and restoration dries the structure.

If my meter in Wheeling keeps moving after I shut the house valve, whose problem is it?

It may be the line between the meter and the house. Call the Wheeling Water Department and ask where its responsibility ends, then call a plumber.
