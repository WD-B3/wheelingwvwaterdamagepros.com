Moundsville is the Marshall County seat, so commercial losses matter here

Moundsville is the Marshall County seat on the Ohio River, about 13 miles south of Wheeling by WV 2. Its population is around 7,700, the largest of the nearby towns we serve. That size means a mix of homes, small shops, offices and public buildings, and water damage restoration in Moundsville, WV 26041, includes commercial work as well as house calls.

When water is spreading in Moundsville

Water spreading through a Moundsville home or business is an emergency. Emergency water removal is available around the clock, the dispatch line is open 24/7, a crew is sent as soon as one is available and same-day response is offered when available.

In a business, the first minutes decide how much stock and equipment is lost. The cards below are written for both homes and commercial space.

The city water department

The City of Moundsville Water Department lists about 4,500 customers and a connected population of about 15,000, which tells you how much of the surrounding area relies on the system. A water main break, low pressure on a block or a discolored supply is something to report to the department. Any failure inside your building is a plumber and a restoration matter instead.

When a line failure inside a commercial building floods a suite, the shut off valve may be controlled by the building owner. That is another reason to call the owner or manager early.

Sewer backups in larger buildings

Floor drains in basements and lower levels are the usual route for a backup. In larger buildings, a blockage in a shared line can send water into more than one unit. The sewage backup cleanup page describes the safety steps and why porous materials usually have to be removed. Notify the building owner and any neighboring units at once, and photograph each affected area.

What a typical Moundsville call looks like

Calls from Moundsville tend to follow a few patterns. Older basements take on water during heavy rain. Winter cold splits supply lines in unheated rooms. Appliance hoses fail while no one is home. In each case the first questions are the same. Where is the water coming from, can it be stopped, and are any electrical items in the water.

Our dispatcher helps you answer those, then explains what a crew would do. If the loss involves an appliance, our appliance leak page lists the usual failure points and the steps that follow.
