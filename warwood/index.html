Water Damage Restoration in Warwood, Wheeling, WV

A north Wheeling neighborhood of single-family homes with a strong industrial past.

☎ (740) 231-9132.

Warwood in north Wheeling

Warwood is a neighborhood in north Wheeling, and the City of Wheeling lists it in its 1st Ward alongside North Park and upper Glenwood. Wheeling Heritage describes its history. Farmland gave way to housing in the early 1900s, the town of Warwood was incorporated in 1911 and Wheeling annexed it in 1920. The name comes from the Warwood Tool Company, which moved there in 1907.

Wheeling Heritage notes that a historic district nomination has been prepared. A 2026 Intelligencer report says the nomination covers 1,244 structures and describes streets mostly lined with single family homes, mixed with large factories and city infrastructure such as a water treatment plant.

What that means for a water damage call

Single family houses are the usual call here. A water heater that fails, a washer line that splits, a roof leak after a storm or a frozen line in an unheated space can each put water on a floor and into a wall. The steps are familiar. Stop the source if it is safe, remove the water, measure the moisture and dry the structure.

We do not claim any special water problem in Warwood. We did not find flood or drainage records specific to the neighborhood, so this page sticks to what restoration crews do with ordinary household losses.

A note on flooding

Wheeling Heritage says the Warwood Land Company, which sold lots in the early 1900s, promoted the area as less prone to flooding than central Wheeling. That was a sales claim from a century ago. It is not a flood map, and it does not tell you the risk at your address.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center will show what applies to your property, and the Ohio River and nearby streams are worth watching in heavy rain.

Steps while you wait for help

  • Shut off the water supply if you can reach the valve safely..
  • Keep out of standing water near outlets, cords and appliances..
  • Lift small items and papers off wet floors..
  • Take photos of the damage and the source..
  • Do not use an ordinary vacuum on a wet floor..

For a burst or frozen line, the burst pipe page and the frozen pipe page list the steps in more detail.

Drying after a leak

Once standing water is removed, the work moves to drying. Air movers push air across wet surfaces, dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air and a moisture meter tracks progress. Wet carpet pad and soaked insulation are often removed, while solid wood trim may dry in place. The carpet page explains the carpet and pad decision.

Drying can take several days. A crew should tell you the plan, the equipment and when readings will be taken.

Insurance and records

Photos, receipts and moisture readings help you describe a loss. Coverage depends on the cause of damage and on your policy, and we do not promise what an insurer will pay. If you are not sure whether your policy covers something, ask your insurer before work starts.

What to tell the dispatcher

A clear description saves time. Say where the water came from, which rooms are wet and roughly how deep it is. Say whether the power is on, whether anyone smells gas and whether the water looks clean, cloudy or dark. If you know your insurer, mention it. Even a rough answer helps the crew bring the right equipment.

Questions from Warwood residents

Is Warwood inside the city of Wheeling?

Yes. Wheeling Heritage says it was annexed in 1920, and the city lists it in its 1st Ward.

Does Warwood have a known flooding problem?

We did not find neighborhood-specific flood records. Check your address with the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

What are common causes of water damage in single family houses?

Failed water heaters, appliance hoses, split pipes and roof leaks are common household causes.

How long does drying take?

Often several days, depending on materials and readings. The crew should explain the plan.

Is a historic district nomination the same as a listing?

No. A nomination is a proposal. Ask the city what review, if any, applies to your property.
