Flood damage cleanup in Wheeling, WV

Flood damage cleanup is the removal and restoration work after river, creek, groundwater or surface runoff enters a building. In Wheeling, WV, that means water from the Ohio River, Wheeling Creek and the small hollows that drain into them. Flood water is treated as contaminated, which changes the safety rules and the list of materials that can be kept.

This page is about rising or surface water. Wind driven rain through a roof or siding is covered on the storm damage page, and a below-grade room has its own page for basements.

When flood water is an emergency

Flood water is an emergency as soon as it enters the living space. Call the 24/7 dispatch line when it is safe to do so, including at night and on weekends. Same-day response is offered when available.

  • Stay out of standing flood water. It can carry sewage, fuel and sharp debris and can hide live electrical hazards..
  • Do not turn on power in a flooded area. Call the utility or 911 about electrical or gas risks before you call a restoration line..
  • Leave the area if the water is still rising..
  • Photograph the high water line and damaged items from a safe place..
  • Do not run a household vacuum or fans in flood water..

What the Ohio River has done here

Wheeling has flooded before, and the history is well documented. According to the West Virginia Encyclopedia, the Ohio River crested at a record 55.2 feet in March 1936, more than 15 feet above flood stage, and Wheeling Island was submerged and its roughly 10,000 residents were ordered out. The river reached nearly 47 feet again in January 1937.

More recently, local reporting based on Wheeling-Ohio County Emergency Management figures said the river crested at 41.85 feet in April 2024, just under the 42-foot major flood stage and the highest level since 2005. The practical point for a homeowner is simple. Low lying streets on Wheeling Island, along the river and along creek bottoms are the places where water arrives first, so check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask the City of Wheeling floodplain manager about your lot.

How flood restoration works

The job usually moves through five steps. Crews assess the water category and the safety hazards, remove the water with pumps and extraction units, remove materials that cannot be cleaned, clean and treat surfaces, then dry the structure to a normal moisture reading.

Material.Usual result after flood water.Notes.
Carpet and pad.Removed.Contaminated water makes these hard to clean safely.
Drywall.Cut out above the water line.Flood water wicks upward, so the cut goes higher than the waterline.
Hardwood floors.Case by case.Some can be dried after cleaning, many cannot.
Furniture and contents.Cleaned or discarded.Porous items like upholstery are often lost.
Framing and concrete.Cleaned, dried and treated.These dry well when exposed.

Industry practice for water damage restoration is set out in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. It is reasonable to ask any company you hire whether its crews work to that standard and how they document moisture readings.

Flood damage versus other water losses

The difference between a flood and a plumbing leak is contamination. A broken supply line puts clean water in the house. Rising river or surface water arrives carrying dirt, oil and whatever it passed through. That difference raises the cost of cleanup but also decides the safe route, so never treat flood water like a spill.

If your home has a finished basement, a first floor near the river or a crawl space that took water, those parts of the structure are usually where cleanup starts. See the crawl space page for the under-floor void.

Cost factors and flood insurance

Price is driven by the depth and duration of the water, the number of rooms, the materials that must be removed, whether items need to be packed out and how many days of drying are required.

Flood damage is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy and needs separate flood coverage. The West Virginia Offices of the Insurance Commissioner directs people to the National Flood Insurance Program for flood coverage questions. Check your policy and ask your insurer or agent what you have. For a scope and price discussion, call (740) 231-9132.

Questions about Flood Damage Cleanup in Wheeling

Do you handle flood emergencies at night or on weekends in Wheeling?

Yes. Flood events do not follow business hours, and the dispatch line is open 24/7. Stay out of the water, then call. A crew is sent as soon as one is available and same-day response is offered when available.

Is flood water treated as sewage?

Not exactly, but it is treated as contaminated. It can carry sewage, chemicals and debris, so porous materials soaked in it are usually removed.

Can I keep my carpet after a flood?

Usually not. Carpet and pad that sat in flood water are normally discarded because they cannot be reliably cleaned.

How do I find out if my address is in a flood zone?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or ask the City of Wheeling Building Code Services office, which lists a floodplain manager and a floodplain map.

Does homeowners insurance cover flooding?

Flood damage is usually excluded from a standard policy and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
