North Wheeling and its Old Town district

North Wheeling is a neighborhood the City of Wheeling lists in its 2nd Ward, together with Wheeling Island, Downtown and Fulton. Part of it is the North Wheeling Historic District, a 2.5 block area known as Old Town, listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1988 with boundary increases in 2008 and 2022. It is roughly bounded by Main Street Terrace, Market Street, Interstate 70 and North Main Street.

The listing describes the district as predominantly residential, with 134 contributing buildings, most from the mid to late 19th century, in Greek Revival, Italianate and Late Victorian styles. Those are the houses where a water loss needs care, because original features are part of what makes the buildings valuable.

Historic review comes before some repairs

The Intelligencer reports that North Wheeling is one of three Design Review Districts in the city. Visible exterior work there needs a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Historic Landmarks Commission, while interior work does not need special review. That matters after a roof leak or storm, when windows, siding or roof coverings may need replacement.

Emergency drying and interior cleanup can start without that review. Ask the city before ordering exterior materials, and confirm permit needs with Building Code Services. We cannot decide those for you, and the rules may change, so confirm them before work.

Water damage in 19th century buildings

Older buildings can hide moisture behind trim, inside walls and under floors that have been covered over more than once. A crew uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to follow the water instead of cutting blindly. Photos before removal create a record of what was there, which helps if original materials need to be matched later.

A ceiling stain often traces to a roof or a bath above. The ceiling water damage page explains how stains, sagging and drying are judged, and the storm water page covers roof and window leaks after heavy rain.

The Ohio River and its history of floods are part of Wheeling, and the 1936 record crest of 55.20 feet at Wheeling is documented by the West Virginia Encyclopedia, but that is a citywide fact. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

Safe first steps

  1. Call 911 for gas smells, electrical danger or rising water..
  2. Shut off the water supply only if it is safe and reachable..
  3. Stay out from under a sagging ceiling..
  4. Keep clear of standing water near outlets and cords..
  5. Photograph the damage and the source before moving anything..

Do not try to dry contaminated water with fans or an ordinary vacuum. Call and ask.

Insurance and paperwork

Insurers often ask for photos, a scope of work and proof of drying. If your building is in a historic district, tell your insurer and your contractor early, since matching original materials can change cost and timing. Coverage depends on your policy and the cause of the loss, and we do not promise any claim result.

What to tell the dispatcher

Describe where the water came from, which rooms are wet and how long it has been there. Mention whether the building is in the historic district, whether the power is on and whether the water looks clean or dark. That helps the crew bring suitable equipment and plan around original features.

Questions from North Wheeling residents

Is North Wheeling a Design Review District?

The Intelligencer reports that it is one of three in the city, where visible exterior work needs a Certificate of Appropriateness.

Does interior drying need historic review?

The same report says interior work does not need special review. Confirm with the city for your project.

Can I start emergency drying right away?

Emergency water removal and drying are typically done first. Ask the city about later exterior repairs.

Has North Wheeling flooded?

We did not find neighborhood-specific flood records. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

Should I tell my insurer the building is historic?

Yes. It can affect how materials are matched and how a repair is scoped.
