Do you cover my address?

If your town is not listed, call anyway. Coverage depends on crew availability and distance, and the dispatcher can tell you quickly.

Why the area matters for water damage

The towns along this stretch of the Ohio River share a lot. Many homes are older, many sit on hillsides with basements or crawl spaces, and the river has flooded before. Winter freezes cause pipe failures, and heavy rain can send runoff toward foundations.

Our flood damage, basement water damage and storm water damage pages cover the conditions that come up most often in these communities.

Travel, timing and what affects response

Response time depends on where crews are, the weather and how many calls are ahead of yours. Hills, bridges and winter roads can add minutes. On a day with heavy rain or a hard freeze, many homes call at once. The dispatcher will give you an honest expectation and safety steps to follow.

Because we cannot promise a specific arrival time, we focus on what we can control. We answer the phone, we explain what to do right away and we send a crew as soon as one is available.

Calling from outside the listed towns

The upper Ohio Valley includes many smaller communities between and beyond these towns. If you live in one, call and give your address. The dispatcher will tell you whether a crew can reach you and what to expect. In some cases we can help with advice by phone even when a crew cannot get there quickly, such as how to stop water, protect belongings and document the damage.

You can also read the water extraction page to see what the first phase of a job involves while you wait.

What to expect when you call from your town

No matter where you live in the valley, the first call follows the same path. The dispatcher confirms your address, asks what kind of water is involved and walks you through safety steps. If the water is clean and from a supply line, the focus is shutting it off and removing it. If it is gray or contaminated, the focus is staying out of it and calling for protective cleanup.

Planning ahead for your town

Knowing where your main shutoff is, and where your sump pump discharges, takes a few minutes and can save hours in an emergency. Walk through your home once a year, check the basement, the crawl space and the appliances, and write the dispatch number where everyone can find it.

How we choose which crew goes where

Crews are assigned by location, workload and the kind of equipment a job needs. A burst pipe in a small apartment calls for different gear than a flooded basement with a foot of water. The dispatcher asks enough questions to match the crew and the tools to the loss, which saves a second trip.

If the first crew needs more equipment, such as extra dehumidifiers for a large area, the dispatcher will tell you when it will arrive. Clear updates matter more than promises we cannot keep.
