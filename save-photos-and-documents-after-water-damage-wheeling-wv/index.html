Saving Photos and Papers After Water Damage in Wheeling, WV

By the Wheeling WV Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

Paper can be saved if you act fast and avoid heat. These are the steps archivists and libraries suggest.

☎ (740) 231-9132.

Paper is time sensitive

Wet photographs and documents start to stick, curl and stain within a day or two. Mold can begin to grow in damp paper in a short time. The steps to save them are simple, but they have to be done quickly, and they have to be done carefully. The guidance below is general. Valuable or rare items should be treated by a professional conservator.

Handle wet paper by the edges, work on a clean flat surface, and wash your hands first. If the water was contaminated by sewage or river water, wear gloves and consider whether the item is worth the risk.

First steps for wet paper

  1. Move the items out of standing water and away from the wet area..
  2. Photograph them where they are, then keep notes about what you moved..
  3. Separate pages and photographs gently only if they are not stuck. If they are stuck, leave them together and treat them as one block..
  4. Rinse mud or dirt from photographs with clean water if needed, holding by the edges..
  5. Lay items flat on clean paper towels or unprinted newsprint to dry, face up, away from direct sun..
  6. Change blotting paper as it absorbs water..
  7. Keep the room cool and the air moving..

When to freeze

If you cannot deal with the items within a day or two, freezing them can stop mold and buy time. Place wet books, documents or photographs in freezer bags, separated by wax paper if possible, and put them in a home freezer. Libraries and archives often recommend this step before professional drying. Do not use frost free freezers for long term storage of important items, since the cycling can damage them over time.

What not to do

  • Do not use a hair dryer or oven on wet photos or documents..
  • Do not lay wet photographs on newspaper that can transfer ink..
  • Do not pull stuck photographs apart..
  • Do not use bleach or household cleaners on paper..
  • Do not leave wet paper in a sealed plastic bag at room temperature..

Digitize while you can

Once items are dry, scan or photograph them. A phone photo is better than nothing. Store copies in cloud storage and a second place. If you have important documents, ask the issuing agency about replacements, since many offices can reissue birth certificates, titles and similar records.

Preventing the next loss

Keep important papers in a waterproof, fire resistant container on an upper floor. Keep a digital copy of key documents. Basements and crawl spaces are the first places water goes, so avoid storing papers and photos there. If you cannot avoid it, put them in sealed plastic bins on a shelf.

See the basement page for what usually happens in a lower level after a loss.

Drying papers and photographs at home

Wet paper and photographs are fragile. Handle them by the edges and work gently. Separate sheets that stick together while they are still damp, and lay them flat on clean paper towels or blotting paper. Change the towels as they soak up water, and keep the room cool with a fan moving air, not blowing directly on the items.

If you cannot deal with them in a day or two, place the items in a freezer bag and freeze them. Freezing stops mold growth and gives you time to decide. Many conservators recommend this for books, photographs and important papers that cannot be dried quickly.

Replacing lost documents

Make a list of what was lost, such as titles, insurance papers, passports and tax records. Most agencies have a process to replace documents, and some waive fees after a disaster. Keep a digital copy of key papers in a secure place for the future, so the next loss is less stressful.

Prioritize what matters most

You may not be able to save everything, so decide what matters. Original photographs and negatives, handwritten letters and legal papers are hard to replace. Printed books and copies of records are easier to replace. Start with the irreplaceable items and the ones most at risk, such as photographs, which stick together as they dry.

Do not use heat, a clothes dryer or direct sunlight. Do not peel apart stuck photographs by force. Soaking them in clean cool water can help release them, and a conservator can advise on valuable pieces.

Local note for Wheeling, WV

Wheeling has a long record of keeping its flood history. The Ohio County Public Library maintains Wheeling history pages that include material on the 1936 flood, which is a reminder that photographs and documents from past floods are valued long after the water is gone. If your family has papers or photographs from older Wheeling homes, they are worth protecting for the same reason.

The same goes for families in Moundsville and Triadelphia, where older homes often hold boxes of family papers in basements and attics.

Common questions

How long do I have to dry wet photographs?

Try to begin within a day or two, before they stick or mold. Freezing buys time.

Can mold be removed from old photos?

Surface mold can sometimes be removed by a conservator. Do not scrub it yourself.

Should I use a hair dryer on documents?

No. Heat can warp paper and set stains. Air dry flat.

Where can I get help with the history of a Wheeling family photo?

The Ohio County Public Library keeps Wheeling history pages, and the library staff can point you to local archives.
