Questions to Ask a Water Damage Company in Wheeling, WV

By the Wheeling WV Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

A few direct questions separate a clear scope from a vague one. Ask them before work starts.

☎ (740) 231-9132.

You are hiring under stress, so use a list

When water is on the floor, you want someone to show up fast. That is reasonable. But a few minutes on the phone can save you from a bad scope or a surprise bill. The questions below are written to be asked in a calm voice, by phone or in person, before anything is signed.

A good company will answer plainly. A company that dodges or pressures you is worth pausing on.

The ten questions

  1. Who will actually do the work, your employees or subcontractors?.
  2. Do your crews work to the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration?.
  3. Will I get a written scope of work with prices before work begins?.
  4. How do you document moisture readings, and can I see them?.
  5. What equipment will be used and for how many days?.
  6. How do you decide what is removed and what is kept?.
  7. Will you coordinate with my insurance adjuster, and what do you provide?.
  8. Who handles permits if the work requires one?.
  9. What does the price include, and what could change it?.
  10. What happens if the readings do not drop as expected?.

What to listen for in the answers

A clear answer to the scope question sounds like a written list of rooms, materials and equipment. A vague answer sounds like a promise. Ask for the scope by email. If the number changes, ask what changed and why.

On standards, a company can honestly say that its crews work to a published standard. A company should not claim certifications it cannot show. We state plainly that licensed and insured contractors do the work, and we do not claim certifications that we cannot document on this site.

Red flags

  • Pressure to sign immediately without a written scope..
  • A promise that the insurer will pay or a claim will be approved..
  • A price that is much lower than others with no explanation..
  • No documentation of moisture readings..
  • A request for full payment up front..
  • Refusal to explain how equipment will be used..

How price should be explained

Item.What a clear scope says.What a vague scope says.
Extraction.Hours or area and equipment.Lump sum.
Drying.Equipment count and days.Flat fee.
Demolition.Which rooms and materials.General demolition.
Monitoring.Number of visits and readings.Not mentioned.
Rebuild.Separate estimate.Bundled without detail.

Mitigation and rebuild are different phases. Ask whether the quote covers drying only or the repair as well. Our own pages on water extraction and structural drying show how each is described.

Insurance and the company you choose

Insurers may suggest companies, but you generally choose your own. Ask your insurer whether there are requirements. Keep a copy of every document you sign, and do not sign a form that gives away control of your claim before you have read it.

Questions about the work itself

  • How will you measure and record moisture, and will I see the readings?.
  • What equipment will you use and how many days do you expect it to run?.
  • What will you remove, and how will you decide what can be dried instead?.
  • How will you protect the rest of the house from dust and moisture?.
  • Who will be on site and how will I reach them?.

Good answers are specific. A company that can explain its method in plain language is usually one that follows a consistent process. Vague answers or pressure to decide quickly are reasons to slow down and compare.

Questions about paperwork and price

Ask for a written scope before work starts, and ask how changes will be handled. Ask what is included in the price and what is not. Ask whether the company will work with your insurer and what documents it provides, such as photos, logs and a final report.

Ask what happens if hidden damage appears. The answer should include a written update to the scope, not a surprise on the final bill.

Questions about people and accountability

  • Are your technicians employees or subcontractors?.
  • Who is my point of contact if something goes wrong?.
  • Do you carry insurance, and can you provide proof?.
  • How do you handle complaints or disputes?.

The answers tell you how the company is organized. Some firms use subcontractors for parts of the job, which is not a problem if the company is clear about it and stands behind the work. Trouble starts when no one is responsible.

Also ask what happens after the drying is finished. You should receive a final report with readings, photos and the dates of service. Keep it with your claim documents and your home records.

Local note for Wheeling, WV

The City of Wheeling requires a building permit for any structural, electrical, plumbing or building alteration in the city, and the contractor is usually responsible for submitting the permit application materials. So one of the practical questions to ask a company is who will handle the permit if your repair needs one. The Building Code Services office is the place to confirm.

If you live outside the city limits, in a place such as Moundsville or Glen Dale, ask your own city or town office about its rules instead of assuming they match Wheeling.

Common questions

Is the lowest price the best choice?

Not necessarily. Compare scopes, not totals, and ask what is included.

Do I need to be home during the work?

Often someone should be present for the first visit and to approve the scope. After that, arrangements vary.

Should I get more than one quote?

If the situation allows, yes. In an emergency, start water removal first and compare scopes for the rest.

If my repair involves plumbing in Wheeling, who pulls the permit?

The city says the contractor is usually responsible for submitting the permit application. Confirm with the contractor and with Building Code Services.
