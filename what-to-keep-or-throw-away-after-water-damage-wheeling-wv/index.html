What to Keep or Throw Away After Water Damage in Wheeling, WV

By the Wheeling WV Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026

Sorting is the hardest part. The water category and the material decide the answer more than sentiment does.

☎ (740) 231-9132.

Two questions sort almost everything

After a water loss, you face a pile of wet things and little time. Two questions help you sort them. What kind of water touched it? And is the item porous or non-porous? Clean water on a non-porous item, such as a glass or a metal pan, is easy. Contaminated water on a porous item, such as upholstery or a mattress, is the opposite case.

Photograph everything before you throw anything away. A list with photos is useful for your insurer, and it helps you remember what you owned.

By water type

Water type.Source examples.What to expect.
Clean water.Supply line, rain, appliance supply.Many items can be dried and kept if handled quickly.
Gray water.Washing machine, dishwasher, sink overflow.Wash and disinfect hard items, discard porous ones.
Black water.Sewage, river or creek flood water.Discard porous items, clean and disinfect others carefully.
Unknown or old.Water that sat for days.Treat as contaminated until checked.

The water types are described in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. See the flood damage page and the sewage page for why contaminated water changes the rules.

By material

  • Carpet and pad: pad is usually discarded. Carpet can be kept in many clean water cases..
  • Upholstered furniture: often kept only if the water was clean and it is dried fast. Otherwise discard..
  • Mattresses and pillows: discard if soaked, especially with contaminated water..
  • Solid wood furniture: often dried and kept, though finishes can be damaged..
  • Particleboard furniture: swells and rarely recovers..
  • Books and paper: freeze or dry quickly if they matter to you, discard if contaminated..
  • Clothing and fabric: launder in hot water if clean or gray. Discard if black water..
  • Electronics: do not plug in. See the guidance on electronics before deciding..
  • Food: discard anything that touched flood water or sat in a flooded refrigerator..
  • Cardboard boxes: discard, since they hold moisture and can grow mold..

How to document what you throw away

Photograph each item or group of items, with labels and serial numbers if you can see them. Write a short list with a rough age and replacement cost. Keep a small sample of carpet or fabric if the insurer wants to see it. Do not discard large items until you are told that the adjuster has seen them or you have permission.

Take photos of the rooms before and after you remove things. A short narrated video works well.

Sentimental items

Photos, letters and family papers matter more than furniture. If they are wet, separate them gently, keep them cool and consider freezing them in sealed bags until you can decide on drying or a specialist. Do not use heat. Avoid peeling photos apart if they are stuck together.

When to let a crew do the sorting

For larger losses, a crew can pack out contents, move them to a dry place and clean them while the structure dries. That reduces the volume you handle and keeps contaminated items separate. Ask whether pack out is part of the scope and what it costs.

Photograph first, then sort

Before you throw anything out, photograph it. Take a wide shot of each room and close shots of damaged items, including labels and serial numbers. Write a list with the item, age and rough value. This record helps with an insurance claim and saves you from trying to remember later.

Sort items into three piles: keep and clean, keep for the adjuster to see, and discard. Anything that touched contaminated water or sewage usually goes in the discard pile, with a few exceptions that a professional can clean. Soft items such as mattresses and upholstered furniture are hard to clean once they have soaked through.

Item.Clean water.Contaminated water.
Solid wood furniture.Often dried and kept.Cleaned by a professional or discarded.
Upholstered furniture.May be dried if treated fast.Usually discarded.
Clothing and linens.Wash and dry.Wash hot or discard.
Books and papers.Freeze and dry if valuable.Usually discarded.
Electronics.Do not power on, have them checked.Usually discarded.

Safe handling while you sort

Wear gloves, a mask and sturdy shoes when handling wet items, especially after contaminated water. Bag discarded items and keep them away from children and pets until they are removed. Wash your hands after handling and before eating.

Do not run a regular vacuum on wet carpet or debris. Do not mix cleaning chemicals. Open windows for fresh air if the weather allows. If something smells strongly of sewage or mold, treat it as discard unless a professional says otherwise.

Local note for Wheeling, WV

Wheeling has a history of river floods that forced families to leave belongings behind. According to the West Virginia Encyclopedia, when the Ohio River crested at 55.2 feet in March 1936, authorities ordered the roughly 10,000 residents of Wheeling Island to evacuate. For anyone in a low area today, the lesson is practical. Keep important papers and irreplaceable items on upper floors or in waterproof containers.

The same advice applies in Wellsburg and Benwood, where river water can reach basements and first floors.

Common questions

Can I keep a mattress that got wet?

If it was soaked, especially by contaminated water, most guidance is to discard it. A small clean water spill that was dried fast may be saved.

What about wet insulation in the walls?

Wet insulation usually has to be removed because it holds water against framing.

Should I wash clothes that were in flood water?

Hot water and detergent are often used for items from clean or gray water. Items from black water are usually discarded.

Where in Wheeling should irreplaceable items be stored?

Upper floors or waterproof containers, especially if your home is on Wheeling Island or near a creek or the river.
